Most conversations about S/4HANA start with the deadline. SAP's mainstream maintenance for ECC ends in 2027 (with extended options beyond), and that date does real work in budget meetings. But "we have to" is a bad reason to run one of the largest projects a company will undertake this decade, and it produces lift-and-shift migrations that recreate twenty years of accumulated cruft on a faster database.

We still recommend the move to most clients. The reason has almost nothing to do with the deadline.

## The platform, not the upgrade

S/4HANA is worth doing because of what it makes possible afterward — real-time analytics on transactional data without a separate warehouse, a clean extensibility model through BTP, and a data foundation that AI features like Joule can actually reach. None of that is available on a creaking ECC instance, regardless of how many bolt-ons you've added.

The clients who get value treat the migration as the moment to retire customizations they can no longer justify. The clients who don't, port everything, and arrive on a modern platform carrying the same constraints they were trying to escape.

## Greenfield, brownfield, and the honest middle

The right approach depends on how much of your current configuration still reflects how the business actually runs. We help clients make that call before the project is scoped — because the decision drives everything downstream — and we'll lay out the framework we use in a longer piece.
