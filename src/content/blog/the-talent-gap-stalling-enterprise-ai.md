When enterprise AI initiatives stall, the explanation offered in the steering committee is usually about technology or budget. Spend enough time inside these projects and a different pattern emerges: the bottleneck is people, but not the people everyone is hiring for.

There is no shortage of data scientists who can fine-tune a model, or engineers who can stand up a RAG pipeline. The shortage is of people who understand the model *and* the SAP order-to-cash process it's meant to support — who can sit with a controller, understand why a step that looks redundant is actually a compensating control, and translate that into a system that works in production rather than in a notebook.

## Two populations that don't overlap

Most organizations have two populations who rarely speak the same language. The AI-native engineers understand the technology but not the domain. The functional consultants understand the domain but treat AI as a black box. Projects fall into the gap between them, and the gap doesn't close on its own.

The teams that succeed deliberately build the bridge — pairing functional depth with technical fluency, or training people across the divide rather than hoping the two sides figure each other out.
