Every major enterprise vendor now ships an AI assistant welded into the product: SAP has Joule, Oracle has its Fusion GenAI features, Microsoft has Copilot threaded through Dynamics and the rest of the stack. The keynote slides are interchangeable — natural language, grounded in your data, agentic workflows, responsible by design. The differences only show up after procurement, once someone has to actually switch the thing on.

We've now stood up all three on live client landscapes. This is what we tell clients who ask which one is "best," which is the wrong question with a more useful answer underneath it.

## The question isn't the model — it's the grounding

All three sit on capable frontier models, and the model is rarely the differentiator. What differs is how cleanly the assistant reaches *your* data and *your* permissions. Joule's advantage is that it already lives inside the SAP authorization model; it inherits roles you've spent years getting right. Copilot's advantage is breadth across Microsoft 365 and Dynamics. Oracle's strength is depth within Fusion's own modules.

The failure mode is the same for all three: a copilot that gives confident answers against data it shouldn't see, or can't see, is worse than no copilot at all.

## Where each one earns its license cost

The honest version of the comparison is unglamorous. It comes down to where your system of record already lives, how disciplined your role design is, and whether you're willing to do the grounding work before you judge the output.
