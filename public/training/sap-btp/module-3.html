SAP BTP Foundations · Module 03 · Administration & Security

Who runs the platform, and who gets in.

Every module so far has used the BTP account someone set up for you. This module flips the seat around: now you are the administrator. You learn how a BTP account is organised, how services get switched on, and how the right people — and only the right people — get permission to use them.

Part 1 · Why this matters

Meridian Books is growing, and the account is becoming a mess.

Until now, one person at Meridian Books did everything in one corner of the BTP account. That worked when it was a side project. It does not work now: there is a team, there are services running, and there is a difference between the version customers see and the version developers experiment on.

Two problems have appeared. First, organisation: experiments and the real customer-facing app are tangled together, and nobody is sure what is running where. Second, access: a new hire needs to use one tool but not touch the others, and right now the only options are "full access" or "nothing."

Both problems are solved by understanding two things: how BTP structures an account, and how BTP handles identity and permissions. That is this module.

Part 2 · Concepts

Structure first, then security.

How an account is structured

You met some of this in Module 1. Here it is again, as an administrator sees it:

🏢

Subaccounts

A subaccount is a self-contained workspace inside your BTP account. Best practice is one per environment — for example a dev subaccount for experiments and a prod subaccount for the live app — so they never interfere.

🎟️

Entitlements & quotas

An entitlement is permission for a subaccount to use a particular service at all. A quota is how much of it — e.g. "two instances." Think: the entitlement is the menu, the quota is how many you may order.

⚙️

Service instances

An entitlement is just permission. A service instance is an actual, running copy of the service that your apps can use. Entitlement = "you may have a database"; instance = "here is your database."

🗺️

You are here

Account → Subaccounts → (entitlements grant services) → service instances run inside. Every BTP screen you see lives somewhere on this map.

How security works

Now the access half. BTP keeps three ideas deliberately separate:

🪪

Identity Authentication

This answers "who are you?" — it is the login. It verifies a person is who they claim to be. It does not decide what they can do.

🔑

XSUAA & role collections

This answers "what may you do?" Permissions are bundled into a role collection — a labelled keyring. You assign keyrings to people, never individual keys.

🤝

Trust

A trust setting is BTP agreeing to believe a particular login system. It is how BTP knows it can rely on the "who are you?" answer coming from elsewhere.

🧠

The mental model

Authentication is the ID check at the door. Role collections are the keyrings handed out inside. Trust is the door agreeing to honour IDs issued by a specific authority.

Why separate "who" from "what"

Keeping login separate from permissions means a new hire can be a real, verified person on day one, yet hold zero permissions until someone deliberately hands them a keyring. That separation is the whole foundation of safe access control.

Part 3 · Guided activity — try this yourself

Run the platform for an afternoon.

Before you start

Entirely in the browser, in the cockpit, using your Module 1 trial account. No terminals. A trial account has limits, so some screens will show fewer options than a paid account — that is expected and fine.

01Take inventory of your subaccount.
⏱ 15 min🌐 In the cockpit (browser)

Open the cockpit and your trial subaccount. Find and open the Entitlements page. This is the list of every service this subaccount is allowed to use.

  1. Scroll the list. For three services you recognise, write down the service name and its quota.
  2. Open Instances and Subscriptions. Notice the difference: this page shows what is actually running, not just what is allowed.

Hold both pages in mind together: Entitlements is the menu, Instances and Subscriptions is what you have ordered.

02Create a service instance.
⏱ 20 min🌐 In the cockpit (browser)

You will turn one entitlement into something real and running.

  1. Open Instances and Subscriptions and choose Create.
  2. Pick a simple, free-tier service from the list — for example the Destination service or another lightweight option your trial offers.
  3. Give it a clear name such as meridian-demo and accept the defaults.
  4. When it appears in the list, open it and look at its details panel.
What just happened

You moved from "allowed to" to "actually exists." That is the entitlement-to-instance step every BTP app depends on — the same move the Module 2 Booster made for you automatically.

03Assign a role collection to a user.
⏱ 20 min🌐 In the cockpit (browser)

Now the access half. You will hand out a keyring.

  1. In the subaccount menu, open Security → Role Collections. Browse the list — each entry is a labelled bundle of permissions.
  2. Open Security → Users and click your own user.
  3. Look at which role collections are assigned to you. Try assigning one more (a read-only or viewer collection is a safe choice), then save.
  4. Remove it again afterwards if you like — the point is to see the assign/unassign action, which is exactly how a new hire would be granted access.
04Find the trust settings.
⏱ 10 min🌐 In the cockpit (browser)

You will not change anything here — trust is a sensitive setting — but you should know where it lives.

  1. Open Security → Trust Configuration.
  2. Read the entries. Each one names a login system that this subaccount has agreed to believe.
  3. In one sentence, write down what would break if an entry here were removed. (Hint: it concerns the "who are you?" answer.)
Part 4 · What you saw

Recap.

You looked at BTP from the administrator's chair. You read the difference between an entitlement (allowed) and a service instance (running), and you created an instance yourself. You handled the security side too: assigning a role collection to a user, and locating trust configuration, the place BTP records which login systems it believes.

The deeper takeaway: BTP keeps "who you are" and "what you may do" as two separate, deliberate decisions. That separation is what lets an organisation grow without its access turning into a mess.

Quick check · 4 questions

1. What is the difference between an entitlement and a service instance?

Show a sample answer

An entitlement is permission for a subaccount to use a service. A service instance is an actual running copy of that service that apps can use.

2. Why keep dev and prod in separate subaccounts?

Show a sample answer

So experiments cannot interfere with the live, customer-facing app — each environment is a self-contained workspace.

3. A role collection answers which question — "who are you?" or "what may you do?"

Show a sample answer

"What may you do?" — it is a bundle of permissions. "Who are you?" is answered by Identity Authentication.

4. In plain words, what is a trust setting?

Show a sample answer

It is BTP agreeing to believe a particular login system, so it can rely on the identity that system reports.

Part 5 · Go further

Extra challenges.

Finished running the platform for an afternoon? These optional challenges go a step further into the administrator's job — provisioning more carefully, planning structure, and thinking about least-privilege access.

How these work

Each challenge below gives you a goal and the basic steps. The finer details are left to you to work out — that is the point. Attempt as many as you like; they are an excellent way to make the concepts stick.

C1Create and document a second instance.
🎯 Challenge

The goal: Provision one more service instance, then write a short record of what you now have running.

How to approach it:

  1. In Instances and Subscriptions, choose Create and pick a different free-tier service from the one you used earlier.
  2. Give it a clear, descriptive name such as meridian-demo-2.
  3. For both of your instances, write two or three sentences: what the service is, what Meridian Books could use it for, and which entitlement it consumed.
  4. Confirm each instance also appears, as expected, on the Entitlements page.
C2Plan a dev / prod split.
🎯 Challenge

The goal: Meridian Books wants experiments kept away from the live customer app. Design the target account structure on paper.

How to approach it:

  1. Sketch the BTP account with two subaccounts: a dev one and a prod one.
  2. For each subaccount, list the two or three services it would need entitled.
  3. Write down one concrete problem this split removes — something that could go wrong if dev and prod shared one subaccount.
  4. Note which subaccount should have the tighter list of users, and why.
C3Design least-privilege access for a new hire.
🎯 Challenge

The goal: A new hire will only ever view dashboards — never change anything. Decide exactly what access they should receive.

How to approach it:

  1. Browse the Role Collections list in your subaccount.
  2. Pick the single most restrictive collection that still lets someone view things, and write down its name.
  3. Explain in one sentence why handing them an administrator collection “just to be safe” would be a mistake.
  4. Describe, in plain words, the steps you would take to grant only that one collection.
